# Dividing fractions (grade 6)

Division asks "how many groups of the divisor fit into the dividend?"
6 ÷ 2 = 3 because three groups of 2 make 6. The same question works for fractions:
2 ÷ 1/4 asks "how many quarters fit into 2?" Each whole holds 4 quarters, so 2 holds 8: 2 ÷ 1/4 = 8.

Why "multiply by the reciprocal" works: dividing by a number and multiplying by its reciprocal give the same result,
because a ÷ b is the number that, multiplied by b, gives a. For 3/4 ÷ 2/5 we want x with x · 2/5 = 3/4.
Multiplying both sides by 5/2 gives x = 3/4 · 5/2 = 15/8. Check: 15/8 · 2/5 = 30/40 = 3/4.

A picture: draw 3/4 of a bar, then measure it with pieces of size 1/2. One half fits once, and the leftover 1/4 is half of a half,
so 3/4 ÷ 1/2 = 1 1/2 = 3/2.

Common mistakes: flipping the first fraction instead of the divisor; expecting the answer always to be smaller
(dividing by a number less than 1 gives a bigger result); cross-cancelling before flipping.
