# The Pythagorean theorem (grade 8)

In a right triangle with legs a and b and hypotenuse c (the side opposite the right angle): a² + b² = c².
It is only true for right triangles. The hypotenuse is always the longest side.

Why it works, by rearrangement: take a square with side a + b. Place four copies of the right triangle inside it,
one in each corner. The empty space in the middle is a tilted square with side c, so its area is c².
Rearrange the same four triangles into two rectangles of size a by b. Now the empty space is two squares, a² and b².
The big square and the four triangles did not change, so the empty areas are equal: a² + b² = c².

Examples: legs 3 and 4 give c = √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5. Legs 5 and 12 give c = 13.
Finding a leg: hypotenuse 10, leg 6 gives the other leg √(100 − 36) = √64 = 8.
The converse also holds: if a² + b² = c², the triangle has a right angle (builders use 3-4-5 to check corners).

Common mistakes: adding the sides instead of the squares (3 + 4 ≠ 5 but 9 + 16 = 25);
using a leg as c; forgetting the square root at the end; using it for triangles without a right angle.
